The build

Four stages, one rule: nothing sees the future

Every feature is computed strictly from information available before kickoff. Every model is evaluated on seasons it never trained on. That discipline is the actual point of the project — a model that isn't checked this way will always look better than it is.

  1. 01

    Ingest

    19,841 matches pulled from football-data.co.uk — Premier League, Bundesliga, Serie A, La Liga, Ligue 1, 2014/15 through 2024/25. Goals, shots, corners, cards, referees, and closing odds from Bet365 and Pinnacle, loaded into DuckDB.

  2. 02

    Feature engineering

    70 columns built per match: rolling 5- and 10-game form, home/away-specific splits, referee card tendency, league scoring average, head-to-head history — every rolling window shifted by one match so nothing leaks forward.

  3. 03

    Modeling

    An independent-Poisson goal model (the standard simplification of Dixon-Coles), logistic regression, and XGBoost — each trained fresh at every step of the walk-forward split, never touching a season it's about to be tested on.

  4. 04

    Backtest

    Train on all prior seasons, test on the next, roll forward. Bookmaker odds de-vigged to a fair probability. Model probability compared against that fair line — log loss and Brier score, not accuracy, because a well-calibrated 55% matters more than a correct guess.

Results

The market wins both markets

Lower log loss means better-calibrated probabilities. Pinnacle and Bet365's de-vigged lines beat every model we built, on every market we tested — which is exactly what a genuinely efficient market should do to a hobbyist pipeline.

Risk management

The honest finding

No, you can't — and that's the result

Pinnacle in particular is one of the sharpest lines in all of sports betting; beating it with free box-score data was never the realistic outcome. The value of this project isn't a profitable strategy — it's a pipeline that can tell the difference between a real edge and noise, and a risk system that shuts itself down the moment the evidence says to stop.

That distinction — proving an edge doesn't exist, rigorously, rather than quietly overfitting until it looks like one does — is the harder and more useful skill to demonstrate.
